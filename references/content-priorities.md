# Content Priorities — What to Include and What to Cut

The skill's job is to filter signal from noise *for working consultants*. This file is the filtering rule.

## The primary lens

**Ask first: would a working senior consultant want to know this tool or development exists?**

If yes — because it changes what they can produce in a day, or how they price work, or what they should propose to clients — it belongs. If no, cut it.

This is the correction from the original framing of this skill. Earlier versions over-weighted enterprise-strategy news (deployment companies, governance frameworks) and under-weighted the actual tool releases. The consultant audience needs both — but the tools are the lead.

## Required coverage per issue

Every issue must cover, where releases exist in the window:

1. **Model releases** from each of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google — by version number *(GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.7, Gemini 3.1 Pro/Flash/Flash-Lite, Imagen 4, Veo 3, etc.)*. Even incremental releases matter because they shift the default baseline consultants work against.
2. **New products and product lines** from each lab *(Claude Design, ChatGPT Images 2.0, Codex desktop, Gemini Enterprise capabilities)*.
3. **Office and productivity-tool integrations** *(Claude for Excel, native operations, shared context across apps, MCP connectors to S&P/PitchBook/etc., Workspace tie-ins, Microsoft Copilot moves)*.
4. **Third-party AI tool releases** that consultants use directly *(Cursor, Perplexity, GitHub Copilot, NotebookLM, Granola, Notion AI, Otter, Fathom, DeepL, design tools)*.

If a category is empty for the window, say so explicitly — don't pad with marginal items.

## Prioritize (in roughly this order)

1. **New tools and product launches** consultants can use directly *(this is the lead)*.
2. **Integration updates** — AI moving into existing tools (Excel, PowerPoint, Workspace, Notion, Linear, browsers).
3. **Model releases** that materially shift baseline capability or pricing — long context, cheaper tiers, new modalities.
4. **MCP and interoperability** — connectors to professional data sources are unusually high-leverage.
5. **Token economics and pricing** that affects how clients buy or how consultants price work.
6. **Governance and regulation** that forces architecture or operating model changes *(EU AI Act, sectoral rules)*.
7. **Major vendor business moves** *(deployment companies, big partnerships, acquisitions)* — these matter but stay in a single section, not spread across the doc.

## Deprioritize

- Pure consumer features with no professional-adjacent use *(novelty modes, social-app integrations)*.
- Raw benchmark improvements without operational consequence.
- Viral AI news cycles, founder essays, podcast hot takes.
- AGI commentary and timeline speculation.
- Architecture details and training recipes — unless they're load-bearing for an operational implication.

## Items worth actively hunting for each month

These don't always make headlines but often matter more:

- **Releases of cheaper model tiers** *(Flash-Lite, Lite previews, free tiers)* — these change what consultants can use casually.
- **New connectors to financial data sources** *(S&P, PitchBook, FactSet, Moody's, LSEG, Daloopa)* — load-bearing for diligence and equity research.
- **Tool releases from outside the big three** *(Cursor, Perplexity, Notion, Cognition/Devin, Replit)* — these often ship faster than the labs and are where individual productivity gains compound.
- **Office integration depth changes** *(native operations vs. add-in vs. assistant)* — depth matters more than presence.
- **Pricing model changes** *(seat-based, consumption-based, per-task)* — affects procurement and client billing logic.

## Volume guidance for a 2,000–3,000 word briefing

- **3–5 sections** (lab vendors + "Other Tools" + Business/Regulation)
- **12–15 development entries total**, ~135–235 words each
- **1 meta-trend** in ~230–260 words

Fewer than 12 entries usually means you missed releases — go back and check coverage of each lab and the third-party tool list. More than 15 means you kept items that don't earn their place.

## When the period is quiet

Some windows produce less new tooling. That's fine — call it. A briefing that says "Anthropic and Google were the active labs this month; OpenAI was quiet outside [X]" is more useful than padded coverage.

## The selection test (final gate)

Before keeping any development, ask:
1. Can a consultant use this directly, or does it materially change what they should propose to clients?
2. Can you write a sharp, specific "Why you care" in one or two sentences?

Both yes → keep. Either no → cut.
